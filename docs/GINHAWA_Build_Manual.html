Stage 0read first

Before you start

Read this stage once from top to bottom. It covers the four skills the whole build needs: staying safe, using a multimeter, soldering, and preparing wires.

The station in one picture

You are building a box that measures the air, runs on a battery, recharges itself from the sun, and sends readings to the Ginhawa app over Wi-Fi or 4G. The picture below is the whole station. Every stage in this manual builds one part of it.

Safety rules

Lithium batteries can burn if shorted

  • Never let a bare red and black battery wire touch each other. Wrap loose battery wire ends in electrical tape until they are soldered.
  • Never solder directly onto a battery cell. The cells only go in the holder.
  • Insert the cells last, only when the stage tells you to. Take them out before you solder anything on the power path.
  • Work on a wooden or ceramic surface, never on a bed or carpet. Keep a metal tray or sand nearby for a cell that gets hot.
  • If a cell gets hot, swells or smells sweet: take it out with pliers, put it outside on concrete, and do not use it again.

Soldering safety

  • The iron tip is about 350 °C. Always put it back in its stand. Unplug it when you leave the table.
  • Solder smoke irritates: open a window or use a fan blowing the smoke away from your face.
  • Wear glasses when you cut component legs. Wash your hands after soldering.

How to use the multimeter

You only need two settings. Use them at every Check box in this manual.

  • Voltage (V⎓, DC, range 20): the circuit is ON. Touch red to + and black to − (or GND). Read the number.
  • Continuity (the speaker or •))) symbol): the circuit is OFF (cells out, fuse out). Touch the probes to two points. A beep means they are connected.

How to solder (5 steps)

  1. Heat the iron to about 350 °C. Wipe the tip on a damp sponge or brass wool, then melt a little solder on the tip. A shiny tip conducts heat well.
  2. Touch the tip so it heats both the pad and the wire or pin at the same time. Count 1–2 seconds.
  3. Feed solder into the joint (on the opposite side from the tip, not onto the tip). Stop when the pad is covered: about 1 second.
  4. Remove the solder first, then the iron. Do not move the part for 3 seconds while it cools.
  5. Look at the joint and compare it with Figure 3. Cut extra leg length with the side cutter.

How to prepare a wire

  1. Cut the wire a little longer than needed (about 2 cm extra).
  2. Strip 5 mm of insulation off each end (wire stripper or carefully with the side cutter).
  3. Twist the strands together, then tin the end: heat it and let it soak up a little solder. A tinned end is easy to solder and never frays.
  4. Slide heat-shrink onto the wire before soldering the second end. After soldering, slide it over the joint and shrink it with a hair dryer on high or a heat gun.
  5. Mark both ends of each signal wire with the same colour of heat-shrink (yellow = SDA, green = SCL, blue/cyan = modem). You only have red and black wire, so the tags tell the wires apart.

Colours in this manual

Red = battery +, black = GND (ground), orange = 3.3 V, purple = 5 V. Use red wire for every + and black for every GND. For signal wires use any wire with coloured tags.

Stage 215 min

Prepare the battery cells

Cells connected in parallel must start at the same voltage. Otherwise the full cell pushes a large current into the empty one the moment they touch.

  1. Measure each of the 4 Beston cells (multimeter on V⎓ 20). Write the voltage on a piece of masking tape stuck to each cell.
  2. Pick the 3 cells whose voltages are closest together. The 4th is your spare: store it in a dry box, away from metal.
  3. If the 3 cells are all within 0.05 V of each other, go to Stage 3.
  4. If not: build Stages 3 to 6 without the cells. Then put only one cell in the holder and let the solar panel charge it until it reads 4.15–4.20 V. Repeat for each cell. When all 3 read the same, put them in together. (The CN3791 stops charging at 4.2 V by itself.)

Check

All 3 cells within 0.05 V of each other, for example 3.71 V, 3.72 V and 3.74 V.

Stage 345 min

Build the battery pack

Connect the holder to the BMS. The BMS cuts the power if the battery is over-charged, over-drained or shorted.

  1. With no cells in the holder, cut the holder's thin leads to 5 cm. Solder an 18 AWG red wire (10 cm) to the red lead and an 18 AWG black wire (10 cm) to the black lead. Cover both joints with heat-shrink.
  2. Look at the BMS. Find the pads printed B+, B−, P− and maybe P+. Cut its nickel strips to about 5 mm (you will solder to them, not weld).
  3. Solder the holder's red wire to B+ and the holder's black wire to B−.
  4. Solder an 18 AWG red wire (15 cm) to P+ (or to B+ if there is no P+). This goes to the fuse in Stage 5.
  5. Solder an 18 AWG black wire (15 cm) to P−. This is the station's main GND wire. Wrap both loose ends in tape.
  6. Put the 3 matched cells in the holder: flat + end against the metal plates, − end against the springs.

Stage 430 min

Make the power board

A small perfboard with two bus wires: red for VBAT, black for GND. All modules take their power from it.

  1. Take the 5 × 7 cm perfboard.
  2. Cut two pieces of 18 AWG wire, each 7 cm. Strip the whole insulation off and tin them: these are the bus wires.
  3. Lay the first bare wire along the top row of holes and solder it every 4–5 holes. This is the VBAT bus. Mark it with red tape or a red marker.
  4. Lay the second along the bottom row and solder it the same way. This is the GND bus. Mark it black.
  5. Each wire that connects later goes through a hole next to its bus and is soldered to the bus wire. Space the joints at least 3 holes apart.

Stage 540 min

Fuse and battery meter (INA219)

The fuse protects the wiring from a short circuit. The INA219 measures the battery voltage and current for the app.

Follow the right half of Figure 5.

  1. Solder the INA219's green screw terminal onto the board (2 pins, labelled VIN+ and VIN−). Then solder the 6-pin header strip on its other edge.
  2. Cut one wire of the inline fuse holder in the middle. You now have two ends: strip and tin both.
  3. Join the BMS P+ wire to one fuse-holder end: twist, solder, cover with heat-shrink.
  4. Screw the other fuse-holder end into the INA219 VIN+ terminal. Tighten firmly.
  5. Screw a new 18 AWG red wire (10 cm) into VIN−. Solder its other end to the VBAT bus.
  6. Solder the BMS P− black wire to the GND bus.
  7. Leave the fuse out of the holder. From now on, the fuse is the station's ON/OFF switch.

Check: cells in, then insert the 5 A fuse

Stage 640 min

Solar panel and charger (CN3791)

The charger takes 6 V from the panel and refills the battery, stopping at 4.2 V.

  1. Cut the plug off the solar panel cable, leaving as much cable as possible. Strip and tin the two wires.
  2. In sunlight, measure the two panel wires to find + (the meter shows a positive number when red probe is on +). Mark + with red tape.
  3. Take one of the plug cables that came with the CN3791. Plug it into a SOLAR socket and check which wire matches the + printed on the board.
  4. Solder the panel + to that cable's + wire and the panel − to its − wire. Cover with heat-shrink, then wrap with electrical tape (this joint will be near the outside).
  5. Plug a second cable into a BAT socket. Solder its + wire to the VBAT bus and its − wire to the GND bus.
  6. Insert the cells and the fuse. Put the panel in the sun.

Stage 720 min

3.3 V converter (TPS63020)

Makes a steady 3.3 V for the ESP32 and the INA219, whether the battery is full (4.2 V) or low (3.0 V).

  1. Pull the fuse out (power off).
  2. Solder a 22 AWG red wire from TPS63020 VIN to the VBAT bus, and a black wire from its input GND to the GND bus.
  3. Solder a 22 AWG red wire (20 cm, tag it orange) to VOUT and a black wire (20 cm) to the output GND. Leave their other ends free and taped for now.
  4. Insert the fuse and measure VOUT to GND.

Stage 820 min

5 V converter (MT3608)

Makes 5 V for the SEN55 sensor. It arrives set to a random voltage, often 12 V or more, which would destroy the sensor.

  1. Pull the fuse out. Solder MT3608 VIN+ to the VBAT bus and VIN− to the GND bus (22 AWG).
  2. Solder a 22 AWG red wire (20 cm, tag it purple) to VOUT+ and a black wire (20 cm) to VOUT−. Tape the free ends.
  3. Insert the fuse. Put the multimeter on VOUT+ and VOUT−.
  4. With a small screwdriver, turn the brass screw on the blue part counter-clockwise (↺). The voltage drops. It can take 10–20 turns before it starts to move.
  5. Stop at 5.0 V (between 4.95 and 5.05 V). If you go too low, turn clockwise a little.
  6. Put a drop of nail polish or hot glue on the screw so it cannot turn by vibration.

Check

Measure betweenMultimeter onExpected
MT3608 VOUT+ and VOUT−V⎓ 204.95 – 5.05 V

Do not skip this step

The SEN55 accepts 4.5–5.5 V. More than that destroys a sensor that costs several thousand pesos.

Stage 91 – 2 h

Make the ESP32 board

The ESP32 plugs into sockets on the larger perfboard, so you can take it out to program it or replace it.

  1. Take the 7 × 9 cm perfboard. Count the pins on one side of your ESP32 (15 on a 30-pin board). Cut two pieces of that length from the female header strip (pull out the metal pin where you want to cut, then cut through the empty slot).
  2. Plug the two headers onto the ESP32's pins first, then set the ESP32 with the headers onto the perfboard. This guarantees the right spacing.
  3. Solder one pin at each end of each header, check everything sits straight, then solder all the remaining pins. Lift the ESP32 off.
  4. Run a bare tinned wire along the left edge (GND rail) and one along the right edge (3V3 rail), soldered every few holes, like the power board.
  5. On the bottom side, connect the header pin under ESP32 3V3 to the 3V3 rail, and the pin under GND to the GND rail.
  6. Solder R3 (4.7 kΩ) between the D21 header pin and the 3V3 rail, and R4 (4.7 kΩ) between D22 and the 3V3 rail. Resistors have no polarity.
  7. Connect the TPS63020 output wires: orange-tagged VOUT → 3V3 rail, its black GND → GND rail.
  8. Connect a thick black wire from the ESP32 board GND rail to the power board GND bus.

Stage 1030 min

Set up the computer and program the ESP32

Install the free Arduino IDE once. Program the ESP32 on its own, out of the board, powered by the USB cable.

  1. Download and install Arduino IDE 2 from arduino.cc/en/software.
  2. Open File → Preferences. In "Additional boards manager URLs" paste:
    https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json
  3. Open Tools → Board → Boards Manager, search esp32, install esp32 by Espressif Systems.
  4. Open Tools → Manage Libraries and install: Sensirion I2C SEN5X (accept "Sensirion Core" too), Adafruit INA219 (accept "Adafruit BusIO"), and ArduinoJson (version 7).
  5. Plug the ESP32 (out of the board) into the computer with a USB data cable. Select Tools → Board → esp32 → ESP32 Dev Module and Tools → Port → COM… (the new one that appeared).
  6. No COM port appears? Install the USB driver printed on the small chip near the USB connector: CP2102 (Silicon Labs CP210x driver) or CH340 (WCH CH340 driver). Or try another cable: many cables only charge.
  7. Open File → Examples → 01.Basics → Blink, add #define LED_BUILTIN 2 at the top, and click Upload (→ arrow).
  8. If it stays at "Connecting.....", hold the BOOT button on the ESP32 until the upload starts.

Check

The small blue LED on the ESP32 blinks once per second. Your computer, cable and ESP32 all work.

Stage 111 h

Connect the SEN55 and INA219, then test

Both talk to the ESP32 over two shared wires called I²C: SDA (data) and SCL (clock).

  1. Cut one double-ended JST GH 6-pin cable in the middle. Plug one half into the SEN55. Do not cut the spare half.
  2. Use continuity mode to find which bare wire goes to pin 1 of the plug (look for the mark next to the SEN55 connector). Write down the colour of each pin 1–6.
  3. Pull the fuse out. Solder: pin 1 → MT3608 VOUT+ (the purple-tagged wire); pin 2 → GND rail; pin 3 → D21 header pin; pin 4 → D22 header pin; pin 5 → GND rail. Cut pin 6 short and cover it with heat-shrink.
  4. Join the MT3608 VOUT− black wire to the ESP32 board GND rail.
  5. INA219 header pins: VCC → 3V3 rail, GND → GND rail, SDA → D21, SCL → D22 (four 22 AWG wires).
  6. Plug the ESP32 into its headers, USB end toward the board edge. Insert the fuse, then connect the USB cable.
  7. Copy the test sketch below into a new Arduino sketch, upload it, then open Tools → Serial Monitor and set it to 115200 baud.
Test 1: I²C scanner
// Ginhawa test 1: finds devices on the I2C bus (SDA = D21, SCL = D22)
#include <Wire.h>

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  delay(1000);
  Wire.begin();  // SDA 21, SCL 22
}

void loop() {
  Serial.println("Scanning...");
  int found = 0;
  for (byte addr = 1; addr < 127; addr++) {
    Wire.beginTransmission(addr);
    if (Wire.endTransmission() == 0) {
      Serial.printf("  found 0x%02X", addr);
      if (addr == 0x40) Serial.print("  <- INA219 (battery meter)");
      if (addr == 0x69) Serial.print("  <- SEN55 (air sensor)");
      Serial.println();
      found++;
    }
  }
  if (found == 0) Serial.println("  nothing found: check SDA/SCL, power and GND");
  Serial.println();
  delay(3000);
}

Check

The Serial Monitor shows found 0x40 <- INA219 and found 0x69 <- SEN55. You may also hear the SEN55 fan start briefly.

USB and battery together

Having the USB cable and the fuse in at the same time is fine for these short tests. When the station is finished, unplug USB for good: the station runs only from its battery.

Stage 121 – 2 h

Connect the 4G modem (A7670SA) and test

The modem sends readings over 4G when there is no Wi-Fi, and its built-in GPS gives the station's location.

  1. Compare your board with Figure 12. Find these pins: VBAT (or VCC 3.4–4.2 V), GND, TXD, RXD, PWRKEY (or PWR, PEN, K). Some boards also have VCCIO or 3V3 for the signal level.
  2. Board has only a 5 V input and no VBAT pin? Stop here and send a clear photo of both sides of the board before wiring.
  3. Pull the fuse out. Solder an 18 AWG red wire from the VBAT bus to the modem VBAT, and an 18 AWG black wire from the GND bus to the modem GND. Keep these two wires short (under 15 cm).
  4. Solder the two 1000 µF capacitors right at the modem's VBAT and GND pins: long leg (+) to VBAT, striped side (−) to GND (Figure 13).
  5. Signal wires (22 AWG): modem TXD → ESP32 RX2 pin; modem RXD → ESP32 TX2 pin; modem PWRKEY → ESP32 D4. If the board has VCCIO/3V3, connect it to the 3V3 rail.
  6. Connect the antennas: the LTE antenna to MAIN (sometimes "LTE"), the GPS antenna to GNSS. Press the small round plugs straight down until they click.
  7. Insert a registered nano-SIM with load (cut corner as shown on the slot). Insert the fuse.
  8. Upload Test 2 below. In the Serial Monitor choose 115200 baud and Both NL & CR, wait 15 seconds, then type the commands in the table one at a time.
Test 2: modem terminal
// Ginhawa test 2: talk to the A7670SA. Type AT commands in the Serial Monitor.
// Serial Monitor: 115200 baud, "Both NL & CR".
#define MODEM_RX 16   // ESP32 RX2 <- modem TXD
#define MODEM_TX 17   // ESP32 TX2 -> modem RXD
#define PWRKEY    4   // ESP32 D4  -> modem PWRKEY

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  Serial2.begin(115200, SERIAL_8N1, MODEM_RX, MODEM_TX);
  pinMode(PWRKEY, OUTPUT);
  Serial.println("Pressing the modem's power key for 1 second...");
  digitalWrite(PWRKEY, HIGH);
  delay(1000);
  digitalWrite(PWRKEY, LOW);
  Serial.println("Wait about 15 seconds, then type: AT");
}

void loop() {
  while (Serial.available())  Serial2.write(Serial.read());   // you -> modem
  while (Serial2.available()) Serial.write(Serial2.read());   // modem -> you
}

Stage 141 h

Prepare the enclosure

Drill the box before anything goes inside. Measure twice, drill once.

  1. Buy an IP65 ABS junction box, about 150 × 100 × 70 mm or bigger. Lay all your parts in it first to check they fit with the lid closed.
  2. Mark the holes on the bottom face with a marker: one 12 mm hole for the PG7 gland (solar cable), one 12 mm hole for the vent, and the SEN55 window (42 × 14 mm).
  3. Drill a 3 mm pilot hole first, then the 12 mm bit (or a step bit). Drill slowly so the plastic does not crack.
  4. For the window: drill holes in the four corners, then cut between them with a cutter. Smooth the edges with a file.
  5. Cut the stainless mesh 10 mm larger than the window on every side. Glue it on the inside with hot glue or epoxy all around. No gaps: insects follow air.
  6. Install the gland: body through the hole from outside, nut inside, tighten by hand plus a quarter turn. Install the vent the same way, O-ring on the outside.
  7. Drill four 3 mm holes in the back wall for the ESP32-board standoffs, matching the board's corner holes.

Stage 151 – 2 h

Put everything in the box

Fix every part so nothing can move or touch another when the box shakes on a pole.

  1. Pull the fuse out.
  2. Screw the four M3 standoffs to the back wall with M3 × 8 screws from outside (2 mm Allen key). Fit the ESP32 board on the standoff threads and hold it with M3 nuts.
  3. Stick the battery holder (cells out), power board, CN3791 and modem to the box with double-sided foam tape. Put a zip tie or tape strip around the holder so the cells cannot fall out.
  4. Mount the SEN55 with its two air openings facing down, pressed against the mesh window. Foam tape on its back. Keep it at least 3 cm from the modem and converters.
  5. Stick the GPS antenna to the inside of the top wall, flat side facing up. Stick the LTE antenna flat on a side wall, away from the GPS antenna.
  6. Pass the solar cable through the gland from outside, leave a small drip loop (a U shape) outside, and tighten the gland until the cable cannot be pulled.
  7. Bundle loose wires with small zip ties. No bare metal may touch the box, another board or the mesh.
  8. Put in the cells, then the fuse. Add 1–2 silica gel packs. Close the lid evenly so the rubber seal presses all around.

Check before closing

  • Serial Monitor (USB connected briefly) shows "Uploaded … over Wi-Fi".
  • Gently shake the box: nothing rattles.
  • Unplug USB. Close the lid.

Stage 161 h

Mount it outside

Place the station where it measures the air people breathe, with sun on the panel.

  1. Fix the box to the pole with two stainless hose clamps (or U-bolts) through its mounting ears or a backing plate.
  2. Mount the solar panel above the box on a small bracket, tilted about 15°, facing south. It also shades the box.
  3. Check that the gland and vent point down. Tighten everything once more.
  4. Note the date, place and photos for the paper's deployment section.

Stage 175 min

See it in the app

The station adds itself. There is nothing to type in the app.

  1. Open the Ginhawa app. A new card appears with "Just added" within a minute of the first upload.
  2. After the first GPS fix (up to 15 minutes, outdoors) the station renames itself after its barangay. Admins can rename it in Settings → Admin.
  3. Watch it for a day: PM values update every 5 minutes, VOC/NOx settle after about an hour, and the battery level stays up on sunny days.
