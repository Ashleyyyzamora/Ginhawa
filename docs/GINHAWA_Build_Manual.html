Part 1

Learn before you build

Eight short stages with no work on the real parts. Read them together as a team. Everything later in the manual assumes you know what is in here.

Stage 1read first

What you are building

A solar-powered box that measures the air and reports to the Ginhawa app on its own.

How the station works, in plain words

  1. Sun → battery. The solar panel makes electricity during the day. The CN3791 charger uses it to fill three 18650 batteries.
  2. Battery → everything. The batteries feed one shared power line called the VBAT bus. Every part takes its power from there, through a fuse that cuts the power if something goes wrong.
  3. Right voltage for each part. The battery gives 3.0 – 4.2 V depending on how full it is. Two small converters turn that into a steady 3.3 V (for the ESP32) and 5 V (for the air sensor).
  4. Measuring. The SEN55 measures dust, gases, temperature and humidity every second. The INA219 measures the battery.
  5. Thinking. The ESP32 collects the readings and averages them every minute.
  6. Sending. The ESP32 sends the readings over Wi-Fi. With no Wi-Fi, it uses the A7670SA 4G modem. The modem's GPS also tells the app where the station is.

Stage 245 min

Electricity in plain words

Just enough theory to understand every step. No maths.

Why this matters

When a step says "measure VOUT before connecting" or "+ to + only", this stage is the reason. Understanding it prevents the expensive mistakes.

Voltage, current, power

Think of electricity like water in a pipe:

Voltagevolt (V)water pressureBattery 3.0 – 4.2 V. ESP32 3.3 V. Sensor 5 V. Panel 6 V.

Plus, minus and ground

Every power source has a + (positive) side and a − (negative) side. Current flows out of + , through the parts, and back into −. In electronics the − side is called GND (ground). All GND points in the station are connected together; that is why so many black wires go to one "GND bus".

Polarity means "which way round". The battery, capacitors, the fuse holder's direction (it has none), modules' VIN and VOUT all have a correct way. Connected backwards, most parts are destroyed instantly. That is why this manual uses red for + and black for − everywhere.

Series, parallel, short circuit

Too much voltage destroys parts

Each part has a voltage it accepts. More than that, even for a second, can destroy it. This is why you measure every converter's output before connecting anything to it.

Signals: the ESP32 talking to other parts

Besides power wires, some wires carry signals (information). Two kinds are used:

  • I²C (say "I-squared-C"): two shared wires, SDA (data) and SCL (clock). The SEN55 and INA219 both connect to the same two wires. Each has an address so the ESP32 can call them by name: 0x69 is the SEN55, 0x40 the INA219.
  • UART (serial): two wires, TX (transmit) and RX (receive). One side's TX goes to the other side's RX. The ESP32 talks to the modem this way.

Stage 330 min

Meet your parts

What each part is, what it does, and how to handle it. Lay all the parts out on a table and find each one.

ESP32 DevKit V1

The brain. A tiny computer with Wi-Fi. It runs the Ginhawa program, reads the sensors and sends the readings.

Know: 30 pins in two rows, a USB port for programming, two buttons: EN (restart) and BOOT (used only when uploading).

Careful: Hold it by the edges. Static from your fingers can damage it: touch a metal tap or table leg before handling.

Used in: Stages 17 – 23

Sensirion SEN55

The air-quality sensor: dust (PM1, PM2.5, PM4, PM10), VOC and NOx gases, temperature and humidity. It has a small fan that pulls air through.

Know: One tiny 6-pin connector (JST GH). Two air openings on one side: they must face the outside air.

Careful: Never blow into it, never let it get wet, never block the openings. Most expensive part in the box.

Used in: Stages 20, 26

A7670SA LTE + GNSS

The 4G phone modem. It sends readings over the mobile network when there is no Wi-Fi, and its GPS finds the station's location.

Know: Pins for power (VBAT, GND), data (TXD, RXD) and a power key (PWRKEY). Two tiny antenna sockets: MAIN (4G) and GNSS (GPS). A nano-SIM slot.

Careful: The antenna sockets break if pulled sideways. Never power it without the LTE antenna attached.

Used in: Stage 21

Solar panel 6 V 6 W

Turns sunlight into electricity to recharge the battery. Gives about 6 V and up to 1 A in full sun.

Know: Two wires: red (+) and black (−).

Careful: Do not scratch the face. Keep it face down when soldering so it does not make voltage.

Used in: Stages 14, 27

CN3791 solar charger

Takes the panel's 6 V and charges the battery safely, stopping at 4.2 V when full.

Know: Two SOLAR sockets and two BAT sockets (white plugs). Small LEDs show charging / full.

Careful: Must be the 6 V version to match the panel.

Used in: Stage 14

Beston 18650 cell (× 4)

One rechargeable lithium-ion battery, 3.7 V, 2600 mAh. Three of them together power the station for days.

Know: Flat end with the small ring = + (positive). Flat end without = − (negative).

Careful: Never short, puncture, heat or solder it. Store away from metal (keys, coins).

Used in: Stages 10, 11

3-cell parallel holder

Holds the three cells and joins them in parallel, so they act like one bigger battery.

Know: Metal plates on one side (+), springs on the other (−). Red and black leads.

Careful: Test it before use: it must be parallel (3.6 – 4.2 V), not series.

Used in: Stage 11

BMS 1S 3.7 V 10 A

Battery protection board. It disconnects the battery if it is over-charged, drained too low, or shorted.

Know: B+ / B− go to the battery. P− (and P+) are the protected output.

Careful: Ground everything to P−, never to B−.

Used in: Stage 11

Fuse 5 A + holder

A thin metal strip that melts and cuts the power if too much current flows. It also works as the station's on/off switch.

Know: The coloured blade fuse plugs into the black waterproof holder.

Careful: Never replace it with a bigger fuse or a wire.

Used in: Stage 13

INA219 power meter

Measures the battery voltage and how much current goes in (charging) or out (using). The app shows this as battery level.

Know: Green screw terminal: VIN+ and VIN− (the battery current flows through these). 6 small pins: VCC, GND, SCL, SDA (plus two not used).

Careful: Tighten the screw terminal well; a loose wire gets hot.

Used in: Stages 13, 20

TPS63020 (3.3 V)

Voltage converter. Turns the battery's changing 3.0 – 4.2 V into a steady 3.3 V for the ESP32.

Know: VIN, GND (input) and VOUT, GND (output).

Careful: Measure its output before connecting anything. It must read 3.3 V.

Used in: Stage 15

MT3608 (5 V)

Voltage booster. Raises the battery voltage to 5 V for the SEN55 sensor.

Know: VIN+, VIN− (input), VOUT+, VOUT− (output), and a blue box with a brass screw to set the voltage.

Careful: Arrives set too high. Set it to 5.0 V before connecting the SEN55.

Used in: Stage 16

1000 µF capacitor (× 20)

A tiny fast-acting energy store. It supplies the modem's short bursts of current so the voltage does not dip.

Know: Long leg = +. The side with the stripe = −.

Careful: Has a direction. Backwards, it can bulge or pop.

Used in: Stage 21

4.7 kΩ resistor (× 50)

Limits current. Two of them "pull up" the sensor data lines so the signals are clean.

Know: Two legs, no direction. Colour bands yellow, violet, red, gold.

Careful: Easy to lose: keep them in their strip until needed.

Used in: Stage 17

Perfboard (× 3)

A board full of holes with copper rings. Parts and wires are soldered to it instead of a custom circuit board.

Know: Holes 2.54 mm apart. Holes are not connected to each other until you solder a wire between them.

Careful: Use one for practice first.

Used in: Stages 7, 12, 17

Female headers

Sockets soldered to the board so the ESP32 can plug in and be removed.

Know: Strips of 40 sockets, cut to length.

Careful: Cut by pulling a pin first (Stage 8).

Used in: Stage 17

JST GH 6-pin cable

The cable that plugs into the SEN55.

Know: Plug on both ends: you cut it in half and use one half.

Careful: Very small and delicate. Go by pin number, not wire colour.

Used in: Stage 20

Silicone wire 18 and 22 AWG

Wire for connections. AWG is the thickness: a smaller number is thicker. 18 AWG for battery power, 22 AWG for everything else.

Know: Red = + (positive). Black = − / GND.

Careful: Silicone does not melt easily, which makes soldering easier.

Used in: everywhere

Heat-shrink tubing

Plastic tube that shrinks when heated. Covers solder joints so they cannot touch anything.

Know: Many sizes and colours.

Careful: Slide it on before soldering.

Used in: everywhere

PG7 glands + M12 vent

Waterproof holes for the box. The gland lets the solar cable in; the vent lets air pressure out but keeps water out.

Know: Body, rubber seal and nut.

Careful: Fit on the bottom of the box only.

Used in: Stage 25

Standoffs, nuts, screws

Hold the ESP32 board 10 mm off the box wall so nothing under it touches.

Know: M3 standoffs (10 mm), M3 nuts, M3 × 8 screws (2 mm Allen key).

Careful: Nylon does not conduct: safe near circuits.

Used in: Stage 26

Silica gel + insect mesh

Silica gel soaks up moisture in the closed box. The mesh keeps insects out of the sensor window.

Careful: Replace silica gel when its beads change colour.

Used in: Stages 25, 26

Set up your workspace

  1. A table with good light, by an open window or with a fan blowing solder smoke away from your face.
  2. Cover the table with cardboard or a ceramic tile under the soldering area. Never solder on a bed, carpet or plastic tablecloth.
  3. Iron stand on your writing-hand side, cable behind it so you cannot catch it with your arm.
  4. A small bowl for cut legs and scraps, and a box for small parts.
  5. Keep food and drinks off the table. Wash hands after soldering (solder contains lead).
  6. Keep the cells in their own box away from the workspace until a stage tells you to use them.

Stage 510 min

Safety rules

Read aloud as a team before the first soldering session.

Lithium batteries

  • Never let a bare red and black battery wire touch each other. Tape loose battery wire ends until they are soldered.
  • Never solder onto a cell, puncture it, drop it, or leave it in the sun or a hot car.
  • Insert the cells last, only when a stage tells you to, and take them out before soldering on the power path.
  • Store spare cells in a box, never loose in a bag with keys or coins.
  • A cell that gets hot, swells, hisses or smells sweet: take it out with pliers, put it outside on concrete away from anything that burns, and do not use it again.

Soldering iron

  • The tip is about 350 °C. It burns skin instantly and melts plastic. Always put it in the stand, never on the table.
  • Never touch the metal part to see if it is hot. Never catch a falling iron: let it fall.
  • Unplug the iron whenever you leave the table, even for 5 minutes.
  • If you burn yourself: cool water for 10 minutes. No toothpaste or ice.

Fumes, clippings, drilling

  • Solder smoke irritates eyes and lungs: ventilate, keep your face out of the smoke.
  • Wear safety glasses when cutting legs or drilling.
  • Wash hands before eating.
  • Drill with the box clamped or held firmly on a table, never in your hand.

The station itself is safe to touch

All voltages in the station are below 7.5 V. They cannot shock you. The dangers are heat (iron, short circuits) and the batteries.

Stage 630 min

Skill: using the multimeter

You will use it at every Check. Practise all three settings now on safe things.

Parts for this stage

  • 1 Beston cell
  • 1 AA battery
  • 1 resistor
  • scrap wire, spare perfboard

Tools

  • Multimeter

Before every measurement

  1. Black probe in the socket marked COM. Red probe in the socket marked VΩmA (or V/Ω). Never the 10A socket.
  2. Turn the dial to the setting first, then touch the probes.
  3. Hold the probes by the plastic. Touch metal tips firmly on the metal you measure.

Setting 1: DC voltage (V⎓ or DCV, range 20)

For measuring a battery, the buses, and converter outputs. Power is on.

  1. Turn the dial to 20 in the V⎓ section (straight line with dashes, not the wavy "~" one). On an auto-ranging meter just choose V⎓.
  2. Practice: measure a normal AA battery. Red on the bump (+), black on the flat end (−). It reads about 1.5 (new) or 1.2 – 1.4 (used).
  3. Swap the probes. It reads −1.5. A minus sign always means the probes are the wrong way round. Nothing is damaged.
  4. Practice: measure one Beston cell. It reads between 3.0 and 4.2.

Setting 2: continuity (beep)

For checking that a wire or joint connects, and that two things do NOT connect. Power is off (cells out, fuse out).

  1. Turn the dial to the symbol that looks like a sound wave or diode (•))) or ▶|). Touch the two probe tips together: it must beep. If not, your meter has no beeper.
  2. Practice: touch both ends of a piece of wire. Beep.
  3. Practice: touch two separate holes on an empty perfboard. No beep: the holes are not connected.

Setting 3: resistance (Ω, range 20k)

  1. Turn the dial to 20k in the Ω section.
  2. Touch the probes to the two legs of one 4.7 kΩ resistor (fingers off the legs). It reads 4.6 – 4.8 (that is 4.6 – 4.8 kΩ).

Stage 71 – 2 h

Skill: soldering

Learn and practise on a spare board before you touch the real parts.

Why this matters

Almost every fault in a home-made device is a bad solder joint. One hour of practice saves days of fault-finding.

Parts for this stage

  • Spare perfboard
  • 22 AWG wire
  • 2 – 3 resistors
  • a few header pins

Tools

  • Iron, solder, flux, cutter, stripper, multimeter

Set up the iron

  1. Put the iron in its stand, plug it in, set it to 350 °C (60/40 or 63/37 solder) or 370 °C (lead-free). No temperature control: wait 3 minutes.
  2. Wet the sponge (damp, not dripping) or use the brass wool.
  3. Melt a little solder onto the tip, then wipe it. The tip should be shiny silver. A black, dull tip will not heat the joint: clean and re-tin it.
  4. Re-tin the tip every few joints and before putting it back in the stand.

The golden rule

Heat the parts, not the solder. Touch the tip to the pad and the leg or wire together for 1 – 2 seconds, then feed solder into the joint (not onto the tip). The hot parts melt the solder and it flows over them. Remove solder, then iron. Do not move anything for 3 seconds.

Fixing mistakes

  • Too much solder or a bridge: clean the tip, touch it to the blob, and drag the excess away; or use the desoldering pump (press down, put the nozzle on the melted joint, press the button) or wick (lay it on the joint, press with the iron, the copper soaks up the solder).
  • Dull, grainy joint (cold joint): add a little flux and reheat until it flows and turns shiny.
  • Pad lifted off the board: stop heating that spot; use the next hole and run a short wire.

Practice board

  1. Exercise 1: solder 10 short pieces of wire leg (cut resistor legs) into 10 holes. Compare each joint with Figure 6.
  2. Exercise 2: solder one resistor through two holes, legs bent outward, then cut the legs (Figure 7).
  3. Exercise 3: make a bus wire: strip 7 cm of 22 AWG completely, tin it, lay it along a row and solder every 4th hole.
  4. Exercise 4: solder a 6-pin piece of female header straight. Solder one end pin, check it is straight, then the others.
  5. Exercise 5: strip, tin and solder a wire end onto the bus wire, with heat-shrink (Stage 8 shows how).
  6. Check every joint with the continuity setting: wire end to bus beeps; neighbouring separate holes do not beep.

If the check fails

  • Solder balls up and does not stick: the pad is not hot enough or dirty. Add flux, hold the iron 1 second longer.
  • Solder smokes and burns black: iron too hot or held too long. Lower the temperature, work faster.
  • Iron does not melt solder at all: tip is oxidised. Clean on brass wool, re-tin, or replace the tip.

Stage 845 min

Skill: wires, heat-shrink, headers and connectors

Preparing wires correctly is half of a reliable build.

Parts for this stage

  • scrap 18 and 22 AWG wire
  • heat-shrink
  • a female header strip

Tools

  • Stripper, cutter, pliers, iron, heat gun or lighter

Wire lengths and colours

  • Cut every wire about 2 cm longer than the distance. Too long is easy to fix; too short means starting again.
  • Red for every + and voltage wire, black for every GND. You only have red and black, so mark signal wires at both ends with a coloured heat-shrink piece: yellow = SDA, green = SCL, blue = modem TX/RX, orange tag = 3.3 V, purple/other tag = 5 V.
  • Twist pairs that go together (for example SDA and SCL) loosely: tidier and less noise.

Stage 1015 min

Prepare the battery cells

Cells joined in parallel must start at almost the same voltage.

Why this matters

If a full cell is connected to an empty one, the full one pushes a very large current into the empty one the moment they touch. That damages both and can make them hot.

Parts for this stage

  • 4 Beston cells
  • masking tape, marker

Tools

  • Multimeter

What the voltage tells you

Cell voltageMeaning
4.15 – 4.20 VFull
3.8 – 3.9 VAbout 60 %
3.6 – 3.7 VAbout 30 – 40 %: normal for new cells from the shop
3.3 – 3.4 VNearly empty
below 3.0 VEmpty: charge soon
below 2.5 VDamaged: do not use
  1. Measure each of the 4 cells. Write its voltage on a piece of masking tape and stick it on the cell.
  2. Pick the 3 cells whose numbers are closest together. Mark the 4th "SPARE" and store it in a box.
  3. If all 3 are within 0.05 V of each other (example: 3.71, 3.72, 3.74), they are ready. Go to the next stage.
  4. If not: continue the build without cells. In Stage 14 (charger) you will charge each cell alone until it reads 4.15 – 4.20 V. Then all 3 are equal.

Stage 1145 min

Build the battery pack

Connect the holder to the BMS protection board.

Parts for this stage

  • Holder
  • BMS
  • 3 matched cells
  • 18 AWG red and black
  • heat-shrink

Tools

  • Iron
  • cutter
  • stripper
  • heat gun
  • multimeter
  1. Make sure there are no cells in the holder.
  2. Cut the holder's thin red and black leads to 5 cm. Strip 5 mm and tin both.
  3. Cut two 10 cm pieces of 18 AWG wire, one red, one black. Strip and tin both ends of each. Put a 3 cm piece of heat-shrink on each.
  4. Join holder red lead to the 18 AWG red wire: hold the tinned ends side by side, heat until the solder flows together. Slide the heat-shrink over and shrink it. Same for black.
  5. Look at the BMS. Find the printed pads B+, B−, P− and maybe P+ (use a phone camera zoom if they are small). Photograph it.
  6. Cut the BMS nickel strips to about 5 mm with the side cutter. Tin them.
  7. Solder the red wire from the holder to B+. Solder the black wire to B− (wire-to-pad method, Figure 8).
  8. Cut a 15 cm red 18 AWG wire. Solder it to P+, or to B+ next to the first wire if your BMS has no P+.
  9. Cut a 15 cm black 18 AWG wire. Solder it to P−. This is the station's main ground.
  10. Wrap the free ends of these two new wires separately in electrical tape.
  11. Wrap the BMS in a piece of heat-shrink or electrical tape so no pad can touch metal.
  12. Insert the 3 matched cells: flat + end against the metal plates, − end against the springs.

Stage 1230 min

Make the power board

A small perfboard with two long bare wires: one for battery power (VBAT bus), one for ground (GND bus).

Why this matters

Instead of joining many wires in mid-air, every part simply solders to a bus. It is neater, stronger, and each connection can be checked.

Parts for this stage

// Ginhawa test 1: finds devices on the I2C bus (SDA = D21, SCL = D22)
#include <Wire.h>

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  delay(1000);
  Wire.begin();  // SDA 21, SCL 22
}

void loop() {
  Serial.println("Scanning...");
  int found = 0;
  for (byte addr = 1; addr < 127; addr++) {
    Wire.beginTransmission(addr);
    if (Wire.endTransmission() == 0) {
      Serial.printf("  found 0x%02X", addr);
      if (addr == 0x40) Serial.print("  <- INA219 (battery meter)");
      if (addr == 0x69) Serial.print("  <- SEN55 (air sensor)");
      Serial.println();
      found++;
    }
  }
  if (found == 0) Serial.println("  nothing found: check SDA/SCL, power and GND");
  Serial.println();
  delay(3000);
}

  • M12 vent

  • standoffs, screws

Tools

  • Drill, 3 and 12 mm bits or step bit
  • cutter
  • file
  • masking tape, marker
  • hot glue
  1. Buy an IP65 ABS junction box, about 150 × 100 × 70 mm or bigger, with mounting ears or a mounting plate.
  2. Lay every part inside with the lid on to check it closes. Plan where each goes as in Figure 30.
  3. Stick masking tape on the bottom face. Mark: one 12 mm hole for the gland, one 12 mm hole for the vent, and the SEN55 window (42 × 14 mm). Keep 15 mm from the edges and between holes.
  4. Clamp or hold the box firmly on a table. Drill a 3 mm pilot hole at each centre, slowly.
  5. Enlarge to 12 mm with the step bit (stop at the 12 mm step) or a 12 mm bit at low speed.
  6. Window: drill 3 mm holes in the four corners, then cut the lines between them with a cutter (many light cuts, not one hard cut). Smooth with a file.
  7. Cut the stainless mesh 10 mm bigger than the window on all sides. Glue it on the inside with hot glue or epoxy all around, no gaps.
  8. Fit the gland: body through from outside, rubber seal on, nut tight inside (hand-tight plus a quarter turn). Fit the vent the same way, its O-ring on the outside.
  9. Hold the ESP32 board against the back wall where it will go. Mark its four corner holes through the board, drill 3 mm.
  10. Remove the masking tape and all plastic shavings.

If the check fails

  • Plastic cracked: seal the crack inside with epoxy; if near a hole, use another position.

Reference

Glossary

Every technical word used in this manual, in plain language.

AC / DC
Direct current (DC) flows one way, like from a battery. Everything in this station is DC. Wall sockets are AC: never connect them to the station.
AT command
A text command for the modem, always starting with AT. Example: AT+CSQ asks for the signal strength.
AWG
Wire thickness. Smaller number = thicker wire. 18 AWG carries battery power; 22 AWG is for signals and small currents.
Baud
Speed of a serial connection. The Serial Monitor must be set to the same speed as the program: 115200.
BMS
Battery Management System: the protection board on the battery.
Bus
One wire that many others connect to, so they all share the same voltage (VBAT bus, GND bus).
Capacitor
Stores a small amount of energy and releases it quickly. Electrolytic capacitors have + and − legs.
Continuity
Whether two points are electrically connected. The multimeter beeps if they are.
COM port
The name Windows gives the USB connection to the ESP32 (COM3, COM4 …).
Current (A, mA)
How much electricity flows, in amperes. 1 A = 1000 mA.
Enrollment
The station introducing itself to the app the first time it connects. It happens automatically.
Firmware
The program that runs on the ESP32.
Flux
A paste or liquid that cleans metal while soldering so the solder flows and sticks.
Fuse
A deliberate weak point that melts to cut power if too much current flows.
GND (ground)
The common return path, the "−" side of everything. All GND points in the station are connected together.
GNSS / GPS
Satellite positioning. The A7670SA finds the station's location with it.
Header
A row of pins (male) or sockets (female) at 2.54 mm spacing.
I²C
A way for chips to talk over two wires, SDA (data) and SCL (clock). The SEN55 and INA219 share these two wires. Each device has an address (0x69, 0x40).
LTE / 4G
Mobile data network. The station uses it when Wi-Fi is not available.
mAh / Wh
Battery capacity. 2600 mAh at 3.7 V is about 9.6 Wh per cell.
Module / breakout
A small ready-made board carrying a chip plus everything it needs, with labelled pins.
Pad
A flat copper area on a board where you solder.
Parallel / series
Parallel: + to +, − to −; voltage stays the same. Series: + to −; voltages add.
Polarity
Which side is + and which is −. Parts with polarity only work, or survive, one way round.
Pull-up resistor
A resistor from a signal wire to 3.3 V so the signal sits at a clean "high" when nothing is talking.
Short circuit
+ connected straight to − with nothing in between. Causes huge current, heat and fire.
Solder
A soft metal alloy melted to join wires and parts electrically.
Tinning
Coating a wire end or tip with a thin layer of solder before joining.
UART / serial
A way to send text one bit at a time over two wires, TX (transmit) and RX (receive). TX of one side goes to RX of the other.
VBAT
The battery voltage rail in this station, 3.0 – 4.2 V.
Voltage (V)
The electrical "pressure" between two points, in volts. Measured with the multimeter on V⎓.
